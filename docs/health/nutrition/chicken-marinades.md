# Chicken marinades: the 3 I actually rotate through

Chicken breast is the backbone of my clean-eating lunch/dinner block —
150-200g raw, most days (macros: [foods catalog](./catalog/foods.mdx),
`chicken_breast_raw`). Plain grilled chicken breast is dry and boring
enough that I used to skip it and reach for something worse. These three
marinades are the reason I don't anymore. All three assume 150-200g raw
chicken breast, sliced or butterflied to ~1.5cm thick so the marinade
actually penetrates in the time available.

## 1. Yogurt-ginger-garlic (my default, ~2h)

- 2 tbsp plain yogurt (or Greek yogurt thinned with a splash of water — it's
  thick enough to cling to meat unthinned but I find that clumps unevenly)
- 1 tsp grated ginger, 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1/2 tsp turmeric, pinch of salt

The yogurt's mild acid + protease activity tenderizes without turning the
surface mushy the way a straight lemon-juice marinade does past 30 minutes.
This is the one I default to because it survives sitting in the fridge for
2-4h without over-tenderizing — I can marinate in the morning and cook that
evening without babysitting the clock.

## 2. Soy-sesame-garlic (quick, 20-30 min, don't go longer)

- 1 tbsp light soy sauce, 1 tsp black sesame oil, 1 clove garlic minced,
  1/2 tsp sugar

This one I use when I forgot to marinate in advance and I'm cooking in the
next 30 minutes. Soy sauce's salt draws moisture and seasons fast, but past
~30 minutes the salt starts pulling too much moisture out and the texture
gets rubbery — this is a "cook soon" marinade, not a fridge-overnight one.
The sesame oil is there for flavor, not tenderizing; add it right before
cooking if you're worried about it burning at high heat.

## 3. Dry brine + spice rub (no liquid, when I want a crust)

- Salt the chicken directly (about 1% of the meat's weight — for 200g that's
  ~2g, roughly 1/3 tsp table salt), let it sit uncovered in the fridge 1h+,
  then rub with paprika + cumin + black pepper right before the pan.

No marinade liquid means no steaming in the pan — the surface sears and gets
an actual crust instead of going grey-and-boiled, which is the single
biggest textural upgrade of any of the three. I use this one when I'm
pan-searing rather than grilling; the wet marinades do better on the grill
or in the oven where a dry crust matters less.

## The counter-argument I take seriously

None of these marinades measurably change the macros I log — I still count
150-200g raw chicken breast at 23g protein / 3.6g fat per 100g (see the
[foods catalog](./catalog/foods.mdx)) and don't adjust for whatever clings to
the surface. If I were marinating in
something calorie-dense (a sugar-heavy glaze, an oil-heavy dressing) that
assumption would start being wrong. These three are all low-calorie enough
that I don't think it matters, but it's an assumption, not a measurement.
