# Overnight oats: ratios and pairings, not just "add fruit"

Overnight oats recipes online mostly stop at "oats + liquid + toppings" and
leave the ratio to guesswork, which is how you end up with either soup or a
brick. The base ratio I actually use, and three pairings built on top of it —
each chosen for a specific reason, not just "tastes good."

## Base ratio (1 serving)

- 40g [rolled oats](./catalog/foods.mdx)
- 40g liquid (milk, or half milk half yogurt — straight water works but
  tastes thin)
- 1 tbsp chia seeds (this is doing real work, not decoration — see below)

Chia is the ingredient people skip and then wonder why their oats are runny
the next morning. It absorbs roughly 10x its weight in liquid over a few
hours — that's what turns a loose oats-and-milk slurry into something with
actual body by morning. Skip it and the ratio above will look too wet at
build time and still be too wet 8 hours later; it never thickens without it.

## 1. Protein-forward: whey + Greek yogurt

- Base ratio, but replace half the milk with Greek yogurt
- Stir in 1/2 scoop [whey protein powder](./catalog/foods.mdx) after the
  liquid, before the chia — mixing it in after chia's already absorbing
  liquid gets you clumps

This is the one I default to on training days. Whey mixed cold into oats
doesn't dissolve as cleanly as it does shaken in a bottle, so the order above
(protein into liquid first, chia last) matters more than it sounds like it
should.

## 2. Antioxidant: frozen blueberries + cocoa

- Base ratio + a small handful of frozen blueberries stirred in cold (they
  thaw into the oats overnight and tint everything slightly purple — that's
  normal, not spoilage)
- 1 tsp [100% cocoa powder](./catalog/foods.mdx) mixed into the liquid before
  adding oats, or it clumps on the surface instead of distributing

Frozen, not fresh — frozen blueberries release liquid as they thaw, which
does some of chia's thickening job for you, so if I'm using a lot of them I
sometimes drop the chia to 2 tsp instead of a full tablespoon to avoid
over-thickening.

## 3. Crunch layer: walnuts + black sesame powder, added at serving time

- Base ratio, built the night before with no nuts in it at all
- Walnuts (roughly 20g) and a spoonful of black sesame powder stirred in
  **the next morning**, not the night before

This is the one pairing where timing is the entire point: nuts soaked
overnight in oat liquid go soft and lose the crunch that's the reason to add
them. Anything meant to stay crunchy goes in at serving time, full stop —
this isn't specific to walnuts/sesame, it's the general rule for any topping
where texture is why you added it.

## The counter-argument I take seriously

The 40g/40g ratio is calibrated to my usual mix-ins (yogurt, chia, whey) —
if you're using a much wetter add-in (more fruit, a thinner milk) or skip
chia entirely, the ratio needs adjusting and I don't have a formula for that,
just "add liquid until it looks right the next morning and adjust for next
time." That's a real gap, not false precision — I got here by iterating, not
by calculating an absorption coefficient.
