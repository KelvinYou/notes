# Supplements I actually take (and the two I stopped taking)

Two supplements pass my bar: creatine monohydrate and magnesium glycinate.
Everything else I've tried — pre-workout blends, BCAAs, a fat-burner — I
stopped taking once I actually read the evidence instead of the label.

## Creatine monohydrate — grade A

This is the most-studied supplement in sports science, and the effect size
(+5-10% strength) isn't subtle. There's no meaningful debate on whether it
works; the only real questions are dosing and timing, which don't move the
needle much:

- Straight 5g/day beats a loading phase for anyone not in a hurry to see
  results within a week.
- Timing relative to training doesn't matter much — same-day is what matters,
  not "pre" vs "post" specifically. I take mine with my pre-workout shake
  because that's a slot I never skip, not because the science demands it.

I don't cycle it. There's no evidence cycling does anything except make you
forget to restart.

## Magnesium glycinate — grade B

The evidence here is real but thinner than creatine's: several trials show
improved deep-sleep quality and reduced muscle cramping, but effect sizes
vary more across studies and a chunk of the literature is small-N. I take it
anyway because the downside (cost, mild) is low and the plausible upside
(sleep quality) compounds with everything else in
[nutrient-timing](./nutrient-timing.md) — sleep is the one lever
nothing else here compensates for.

Dose and exact timing depend on the rest of a night's protocol (what else is
scheduled pre-sleep), so I don't fix a number here — see the food/price
lookup for cost: `datasets/nutrition/foods/supplements.yaml`.

## What I stopped taking

- **Pre-workout blends**: the only ingredient with real backing is caffeine,
  and I already get that from coffee. Everything else in the blend is
  underdosed proprietary-blend filler.
- **BCAAs**: redundant if total daily protein is already adequate — see
  [protein-and-energy](./protein-and-energy.md). Free amino acids
  isolated from whole protein don't out-perform the protein itself once
  total intake is covered.

## The counter-argument I take seriously

"Evidence grade A" for creatine is about population-level effect size, not a
guarantee for any one person — a minority of people are non-responders
(don't retain intracellular water/creatine the same way). I haven't run a
personal N=1 washout test to confirm I'm a responder; I'm extrapolating from
the population data like everyone else who takes it without testing.
